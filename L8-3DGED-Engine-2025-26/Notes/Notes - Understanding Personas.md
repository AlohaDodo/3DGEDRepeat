# Team Personas for Reflection

# Preface

Now that you have completed your first substantial team project, these slides present a series of well-recognised teamwork personas. Their purpose is to help you analyse team behaviours, identify patterns you may have encountered, and use these insights to improve your effectiveness in future collaborative work.

The personas are general behavioural patterns found in group projects and professional teams.

Each persona:
- Highlights teamwork behaviours
- Shows how those behaviours affect others
- Includes a growth message for improvement

> :bulb: These personas describe behaviours, not people. They are tools for **reflection**, not **labels**. Behaviours can **change** — and that **self-awareness** is one of the key benefits that comes from working in a team.

---

# 1. The Diligent Developer

Reliable, consistent, technically versatile.  

Keeps the team moving, completes tasks on time, documents, and supports others.

**Growth Message:**  
Expand impact by mentoring others and taking on more strategic responsibilities.

---

# 2. The Communicator

Clear, organised, alignment-focused. 

Prevents misunderstandings, highlights decisions, keeps everyone informed.

**Growth Message:**  
Strengthen assertiveness — challenge poor decisions, not just clarify them.

---

# 3. The Debugging Detective

Calm, methodical, crisis-stabiliser.  

Excellent at tracing issues, fixing problems, and reducing team anxiety.

**Growth Message:**  
Move from fixer to leader by promoting testing and teaching debugging skills.

---

# 4. The Supportive Collaborator

Team-first and generous.  

Helps teammates, picks up neglected tasks, supports group cohesion.

**Growth Message:**  
Balance helping others with self-advocacy and taking on challenging tasks.

---

# 5. The Vision Keeper

Focuses the team on what really matters. 

Prevents scope creep, maintains project coherence, clarifies priorities.

**Growth Message:**  
Strengthen collaboration by integrating others’ ideas into the shared vision.

---

# 6. The Innovator

Creative experimenter with fresh ideas. 

Generates unique concepts, prototypes interesting features, inspires the team.

**Growth Message:**  
Balance creativity with feasibility — refine ideas to fit the team’s goals and capacity.

---

# 7. The Diva Coder

Brilliant but brittle — wants to be the sole expert.  

Takes feedback personally and undermines external authority. Their need to be seen as the technical expert damages trust, slows progress, alienates and/or intimidates teammates.

**Growth Message:**  
Real leadership comes from collaboration, openness to constructive criticism, adapting to an existing system, and shared ownership of the project.

---

# 8. The Joker

Fun in meetings, absent in implementation.  

Lightens the mood but avoids responsibility and deadlines. Lacks self-discipline.

**Growth Message:**  
Build trust through small, consistent contributions completed reliably.

---

# 9. The Ghost Member

Present in theory, missing in practice.  

Attends meetings but doesn’t deliver meaningful work. Deadlines slip. 

**Growth Message:**  
Rebuild trust through steady participation, early communication, and achievable tasks.

---

# 10. The Last-Minute Hero

Dramatic rescues, preventable crises. 

Produces big bursts of work right before deadlines, creating stress and integration issues.

**Growth Message:**  
Shift from last-minute heroics to steady, incremental progress.

---

# 11. The Over-Committer

Great intentions, unrealistic follow-through.  

Volunteers for too much, becomes a bottleneck, slows progress unintentionally.

**Growth Message:**  
Better estimation, delegation, and saying “no” when needed improves reliability.

---

# 12. The Comfortable Specialist

Competent but stays in their niche. 

Delivers within their speciality but avoids tasks that stretch their skills.

**Growth Message:**  
Grow by taking on at least one new, unfamiliar challenge each sprint.

---

# Reflection Questions

- Which persona(s) do you identify with most?
- Which persona frustrates you most when you encounter it in a team? Why?
-  Which growth message resonates with you personally?
- What behaviour will you actively improve in your next team project?
